Your session

A folder. That's the format.

Tape Looper writes plain 24-bit audio as you work: one file per track, stereo pairs written as a single stereo file, and MIX TO DISK for the full performance in one pass. Hit REC TO DISK and the mix is already a WAV. Open the folder in ten years and everything still plays.

And it names them for you. Every file carries the session, the track, its length in bars and the tempo, so a loop dragged into a DAW three months from now still tells you what it is.

Name your sessions, search them, preview them from the drawer, and point the whole library at any folder you like, including one that syncs.

Questions

Before you download.

macOS says the app isn't verified. Is that normal?

Yes. Tape Looper is delivered directly rather than through the Mac App Store, so the first launch takes one extra step. Double-click it, click Done on the warning, then open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to Security and click Open Anyway. After that it opens with a double-click like anything else.

Is this a DAW replacement?

No, and it shouldn't be. It's the sketchpad you reach for before the DAW, the thing that's already recording while Logic is still opening. When the idea is worth finishing, drag the track files straight into your DAW and finish it there.

How does Neural Amp Modeler work here?

NAM sits on the INPUT strip. Load a .nam capture, turn NAM on, and the modeled guitar is what you hear and what gets written to the armed track. Off is DI. Models run at their native sample rate. 48 kHz is the sweet spot. Amp tones are free at TONE3000.

What is mix to disk?

REC TO DISK captures the whole performance to a 24-bit WAV in one pass: every mute, fader move, send and fade-out, exactly as you heard it. It lands in the session folder next to the per-track files, already named with the session and the tempo. No bounce, no export dialog.

What happens to my recordings when the trial ends?

Nothing. Your sessions are ordinary audio files in ordinary folders, and they stay yours whether or not you buy a licence. The app will still open and play them.

Can I use my audio interface?

Any Core Audio input works: built-in, USB interface, aggregate devices. You pick the device and buffer size in the app, and timing is locked to that hardware clock, which is what keeps overdubs sample-accurate through a long session.

Does it work with a foot switch?

Not yet. MIDI foot control (record, stop, insert, undo, next track, all MIDI-learnable) is the next feature on the roadmap. Join the list below and you'll hear when it lands.

Windows? iPad?

Mac only. It's built on Core Audio directly, which is exactly why the timing is what it is. There's no port planned.
